# May 4 ARMS capacity sensitivity and retained-library startup audit

This is a bounded source/ELF audit. It introduces no code changes, benchmark executions, or host changes. The mechanisms below establish that ARMS performance need not be monotonic in free DRAM; they do not identify which mechanism affected an individual run.

## Concrete capacity-dependent policy behavior

The policy reads live node-0 `MemFree`, subtracts 5% of node-0 `MemTotal`, and divides the result into 2 MiB pages. This value is recomputed during each policy step, rather than fixing capacity from the startup “DRAM size” print. See frozen [arms_kernel.cpp](../source/may04/arms_kernel.cpp), `fasttier_free_kb`, `calculate_free_hugepages`, and `rank_scores_for_migration` around lines 1598–1662.

1. **More free space widens ARMS eligibility and preserves page history.** The rank cutoff is currently scored DRAM pages plus free hugepages. In [page.cpp](../source/may04/page.cpp), `update_can_promote` around line 421 increments `hot_age` for positive-score pages inside that cutoff and resets age/eligibility outside it. The migration benefit is `score × hot_age × HF_SAMPLE_PERIOD × latency_diff` in ARMS, whereas the model branch uses its model score without that age multiplier. A larger free pool can therefore admit extra pages and let their migration benefit accumulate. This is an ARMS-specific distinction from the model pathway.

2. **Free capacity changes the work quota.** The policy budgets migration work using promotion-only cost while space remains, otherwise promotion-plus-demotion cost; it then multiplies the quota by ten workers. With the C220G5 minimum promotion/demotion cost of 636 microseconds and a 500,000-microsecond interval, the allowed operation count is 3,930 at zero free hugepages, 6,930 at 600 free hugepages, and 7,860 after promotion-only budgeting saturates. These are ceilings, not actual migrations. The larger capacity may induce extra work rather than merely improve placement of an unchanged migration stream.

3. **Promotion may still be queued without available space or a victim.** In `select_migration_candidates` around lines 1727–1783, the cold-victim search may stop without selecting a DRAM page. The final `benefit > cost` block can nonetheless enqueue the hot page even when `free_hugepages <= 0`. In [migration_worker.cpp](../source/may04/migration_worker.cpp), lines 357–397, workers attempt promotion first, then demotion/retry after failure, sleep one millisecond, and may retry half a batch after ENOMEM. Backoff is zero. Failed work updates the measured migration cost, and intervals with no migration candidates decay estimates by 1.5. This can produce feedback between capacity, eligibility, failure overhead, and future selection.

4. **There is a latent mixed-batch bookkeeping bug.** `log_move_page` around lines 241–279 filters fragmented pages out of `vas` and `status`, but interprets the resulting status vector with `pages[i]` from the original unfiltered vector. For `[fragmented A, normal B]`, B's success can be applied to A's `in_dram` field. This can corrupt later estimates of scored DRAM occupancy and retry selection. The code exists in both frozen May4 and the current source, originating with fragmented-page support in commit `e6223c95a` (March 15, 00:39:26 UTC). It is therefore a possible condition-dependent behavior, not a newly introduced explanation for the archive difference. No surviving trace proves that a relevant batch exercised it.

These mechanisms make additional promotion/failure/compaction activity a meaningful diagnostic when extra DRAM changes timing. They do not predict that more DRAM must hurt: the original higher-memory ARMS runs were faster than the 4 GB reference. Exact per-page migration traces would be needed to establish which mechanism actually ran; host-wide counters can only provide supporting evidence.

## Retained April binary and runner/ABI interactions

The failed attempt's saved command sets a single `LD_PRELOAD` at the final `env ... bc` stage. Its `time`, `timeout`, `numactl`, and `taskset` wrappers are outside that preload scope. No second tiering library is explicitly composed into that command.

Read-only symbol inspection shows that the retained library exports `__libc_start_main`, but no malloc/free, pthread, GOMP, or omp replacements. Its allocation and pthread functions are normal unresolved runtime imports. Comparing defined dynamic symbols with the BC executable finds only one shared definition: standard `std::ctype<char>::do_widen`, whose character-return behavior matches. There is no observed application data/class symbol collision between the two files.

BC was built with GCC 13.3.0; the retained library and the successful freshly rebuilt May4 library both report GCC 15.1.0. The retained file has ordinary libnuma/libstdc++/libm/libgcc_s/libc dependencies and no RPATH/RUNPATH. Compiler-version difference alone therefore does not distinguish the failed and successful cases.

The existing approximate disassembly comparison finds matching implementations of `__libc_start_main`, `open_perf_events`, both IP-range setter functions, and the two global initializers in the retained and fresh May4 libraries. Their global constructor order also matches. These observations weaken a generic runner/OpenMP/ABI explanation but cannot rule out a layout-sensitive startup bug or an unrecorded environment interaction.

The last printed banner is IMC setup; no backtrace identifies whether the allocator assertion happened inside that setup or during subsequent thread startup. In particular, the absence of “Initialization complete” alone does not prove that no worker had started. Apport discarded the crash, as recorded in [legacy_binary/crash_record.txt](legacy_binary/crash_record.txt). The failed attempt is invalid timing evidence. One failed launch does not prove that the artifact could never run under another historical environment, nor does it connect that artifact to the archived benchmark.
